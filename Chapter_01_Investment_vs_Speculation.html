Paragraph 2: "Investor" no sacho arth

What do we mean by "investor"? Throughout this book the term will be used in contradistinction to "speculator." As far back as 1934, in our textbook Security Analysis, we attempted a precise formulation of the difference between the two, as follows: "An investment operation is one which, upon thorough analysis promises safety of principal and an adequate return. Operations not meeting these requirements are speculative."
"Investor" no shu arth? Aa book ma e shabd "speculator" thi alag (viruddh) vaparyo chhe. 1934 ma, Security Analysis book ma, aa be vachche no pharak chokkas rite samjavyo hato:

"Investment e evu kaam chhe je, puri tapas (analysis) pachhi, mool rakam ni suraksha ane yogya vadharo (return) nu vachan ape. Je kaam aa sharto na pura kare te speculative (satta jevu) chhe."

🔑 Aa book ni sauthi mahatvani vyakhya. Investment = 3 vastu:
1. ✅ Thorough analysis (puri tapas karine)
2. ✅ Safety of principal (mool paisa safe)

Aa 3 ma thi ek pan na hoy to e speculation chhe.
Udaharan: Mitra e kidhu "aa share uchhlase" ane tame tapas karya vagar paisa lagavya. Aa speculation chhe, investment nahi.

Ame aa vyakhya ne 38 varsh sudhi majbooti thi pakdi rakhi, pan "investor" shabd na upyog ma moti pherfar aavya. 1929-1932 ni moti bazaar ni padti pachhi badha common stocks (shares) ne swabhav thi j speculative (jokhmi) manva ma aavta hata.

Paragraph 6: Bazaar ni murkhai

PART 2 (Page 21 - 22): Speculation (satta) ne kevi rite handle karvu

Paragraph 1: Speculation kharab nathi, pan...

Outright speculation is neither illegal, immoral, nor (for most people) fattening to the pocketbook. More than that, some speculation is necessary and unavoidable, for in many common-stock situations there are substantial possibilities of both profit and loss, and the risks therein must be assumed by someone. There is intelligent speculation as there is intelligent investing.
Khullo speculation gerkanooni nathi, anaitik nathi, pan (moti bhag na loko mate) khissu bharto nathi. Vadhare, thodo speculation jaruri ane taali na shakay tevo chhe, kemke ghana shares ma nafo ane nukshan bane ni moti shakyata hoy chhe, ane e jokham koi ne to levo j padse. Jem samajdar investing hoy chhe, tem samajdar speculation pan hoy chhe.

Paragraph 2: Speculation ni 3 sauthi moti bhoolo

But there are many ways in which speculation may be unintelligent. Of these the foremost are: (1) speculating when you think you are investing; (2) speculating seriously instead of as a pastime, when you lack proper knowledge and skill for it; and (3) risking more money in speculation than you can afford to lose.
Pan speculation murkhaipurn hoi shake tevi ghani rito chhe. Temathi sauthi mukhya:
(1) Tame investing karo chho evu manta hov ane hakikat ma speculation karta hov.
(2) Yogya gnan ane kaushalya na hoy chhata, shauk ne badle gambhir rite speculation karvu.
(3) Je paisa gumavva parvadi shako tenathi vadhare paisa speculation ma jokham ma mukvo.

Paragraph 3: Margin ane "hot" shares

In our conservative view every nonprofessional who operates on margin should recognize that he is ipso facto speculating, and it is his broker's duty so to advise him. And everyone who buys a so-called "hot" common-stock issue, or makes a purchase in any way similar thereto, is either speculating or gambling.
Amari saavchet drashti e, je non-professional margin par vyavahar kare te pote j speculation kare chhe, ane broker nu kaam chhe ke tene aa vishe chetave. Ane je koi "hot" (garam, charcha vala) share kharide ke tena jevu kai kharide, te speculation ke juga kare chhe.

Paragraph 4: Graham ni salah (speculation karvu j hoy to)

Speculation is always fascinating, and it can be a lot of fun while you are ahead of the game. If you want to try your luck at it, put aside a portion--the smaller the better--of your capital in a separate fund for this purpose. Never add more money to this account just because the market has gone up and profits are rolling in. (That's the time to think of taking money out of your speculative fund.) Never mingle your speculative and investment operations in the same account, nor in any part of your thinking.
Speculation hamesha aakarshak hoy chhe, ane jyare tame aagal chalo chho tyare bov maja aave chhe. Jo naseeb aajmavvu hoy, to tamari mudi no ek nano hisso (jetlo nano tetlu saru) alag fund ma rakho. Bazaar vadhyo ane nafo aave chhe em kari ne aa account ma kyarey vadhare paisa na nakhsho. (Tyare to speculative fund ma thi paisa kadhva no vichar karvo joiye.) Tamara speculative ane investment vyavahar ne kyarey ek j account ma ke ek j vichar ma bheli na deshso.

🔑 Graham no 50-50 niyam (Defensive investor mate):
• Bonds: 25% thi 75% vachche
• Shares: 25% thi 75% vachche (bonds na ulta)
• Sauthi saral: 50% bonds + 50% shares
• Bazaar na farak thi 5% bagde to pacha 50-50 karo (rebalancing)

Paragraph 1: 1971 na ant ma bonds ni kamai

Toward the end of 1971 it was possible to obtain 8% taxable interest on good medium-term corporate bonds, and 5.7% tax-free on good state or municipal securities. In the shorter-term field the investor could realize about 6% on U.S. government issues due in five years. In the latter case the buyer need not be concerned about a possible loss in market value, since he is sure of full repayment, including the 6% interest return, at the end of a comparatively short holding period. The DJIA at its recurrent price level of 900 in 1971 yields only 3.5%.
1971 na ant taraf, sari companies na madhyam gaala na bonds par 8% (taxable) vyaj male tevu hatu, ane sara sarkari (state/municipal) tax-mukt par 5.7%. Ochha gaala (5 varsh) na America sarkar na bonds par lagbhag 6%. Aa chhelli ma kharidnar ne bhav ghatva ni chinta nathi, kem ke nakki vyaj sathe puri rakam pacha male chhe. Jyare Dow 900 par shares par dividend fakt 3.5% hatu.

Paragraph 2: Shares ni kul kamai

If there is no serious adverse change, the defensive investor should be able to count on the current 3.5% dividend return on his stocks and also on an average annual appreciation of about 4%. As we shall explain later this appreciation is based essentially on the reinvestment by the various companies of a corresponding amount annually out of undistributed profits. On a before-tax basis the combined return of his stocks would then average, say, 7.5%, somewhat less than his interest on high-grade bonds. On an after-tax basis the average return on stocks would work out at some 5.3%.
Jo koi moto kharab fer na thay, to defensive investor shares par 3.5% dividend ane sarerash 4% varshik bhav vadharo ni apeksha rakhi shake. Aa bhav vadharo mukhya rite aa kaaran thi aave chhe ke companies vehnchya vagar na nafa ma thi darek varshe pacha dhandha ma paisa lagave chhe. Tax pehla, shares ni kul kamai lagbhag 7.5%, je sara bonds na vyaj thi thodi ochhi. Tax pachhi te lagbhag 5.3% thay.

Paragraph 4: Bonds j saru, to 100% bonds kem nahi?

Consequently we are forced to the conclusion that now, toward the end of 1971, bond investment appears clearly preferable to stock investment. If we could be sure that this conclusion is right we would have to advise the defensive investor to put all his money in bonds and none in common stocks until the current yield relationship changes significantly in favor of stocks.
Etle ame aa nishkarsh par aavva majboor chhiye ke 1971 na ant ma bonds, shares karta spasht rite saru dekhay chhe. Jo ame khaatri thi kahi shakta hot ke aa sachu chhe, to ame defensive investor ne salah apat ke badho paisa bonds ma ane shares ma kai nahi, jyaa sudhi kamai no sambandh shares na pakshe na badlay.
But of course we cannot be certain that bonds will work out better than stocks from today's levels. ... But there is always the possibility--though we consider it remote--of an accelerating inflation, which in one way or another would have to make stock equities preferable to bonds payable in a fixed amount of dollars.
Pan ame khaatri thi kahi shakta nathi ke bonds j saru raheshe. Hamesha shakyata chhe (bhale dur ni) ke mehngai jhadpi vadhe, tyare shares nakki rakam na bonds karta saru bane.

Graham ni 3 shakyata o jyaa bonds 100% galat padi shake:

Paragraph 5: Aakhri salah: 50-50 j rakho

Hence, after this foreshortened discussion of the major considerations, we once again enunciate the same basic compromise policy for defensive investors--namely that at all times they have a significant part of their funds in bond-type holdings and a significant part also in equities. It is still true that they may choose between maintaining a simple 50-50 division between the two components or a ratio, dependent on their judgment, varying between a minimum of 25% and a maximum of 75% of either.
Etle, mukhya vastuo ni tunki charcha pachhi, ame farithi e j mool "samjuti vali niti" kahiye chhiye: defensive investor e hamesha potana paisa no motto hisso bonds ma ane motto hisso shares ma rakhvo. Hajee pan satya chhe ke e saral 50-50 rakhi shake, athva potani samaj pramane 25% thi 75% vachche pramaan rakhi shake.

Paragraph 7: "Bootstrap operation" (khoob mahatva nu)

It may not seem attractive in relation to the 14%, or so, return shown by common stocks during the 20 years of the predominantly bull market after 1949. But it should be remembered that between 1949 and 1969 the price of the DJIA had advanced more than fivefold while its earnings and dividends had about doubled. Hence the greater part of the impressive market record for that period was based on a change in investors' and speculators' attitudes rather than in underlying corporate values. To that extent it might well be called a "bootstrap operation."
Aa kamai 1949 pachhi na 20 varsh na 14% jetli kamai ni sarkhaman aakarshak na lage. Pan yaad rakho ke 1949 thi 1969 vachche Dow no bhav 5 gano thi vadhare vadhyo, jyare kamai ane dividend lagbhag 2 gana j thaya. Etle e samay no prabhavit karnaro rekord mukhya rite investor ane speculator ona vichar ma badlav par aadharit hato, company ni asli kimat ma vadharo par nahi. Aa ma "bootstrap operation" kahevay.
