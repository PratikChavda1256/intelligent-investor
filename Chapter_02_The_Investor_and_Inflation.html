Paragraph 1: Mehngai ni asar kone vadhare?

Inflation, and the fight against it, has been very much in the public's mind in recent years. The shrinkage in the purchasing power of the dollar in the past, and particularly the fear (or hope by speculators) of a serious further decline in the future, has greatly influenced the thinking of Wall Street. It is clear that those with a fixed dollar income will suffer when the cost of living advances, and the same applies to a fixed amount of dollar principal. Holders of stocks, on the other hand, have the possibility that a loss of the dollar's purchasing power may be offset by advances in their dividends and the prices of their shares.
Mehngai ane tena virudh ni ladai haal na varsho ma lokona manma chhe. Ateet ma dollar ni kharid-shakti ghati, ane khaas karine bhavishya ma vadhu ghatado thavano dar (ke speculators ni aasha) e Wall Street ni vichar-sarani ne ghani asar kari. Spasht chhe ke nakki dollar aavak valaa ne mehngai vadhe tyare nuksaan thay, ane nakki mool rakam par pan e j lagu pade. Bija taraf, shares na malik ne shakyata chhe ke kharid-shakti ghatvaano nuksaan temna dividend ane share na bhav vadhva thi bharai jay.

Paragraph 2: "Bonds kharab, shares j saaru" evi mannyata

On the basis of these undeniable facts many financial authorities have concluded that (1) bonds are an inherently undesirable form of investment, and (2) consequently, common stocks are by their very nature more desirable investments than bonds. We have heard of charitable institutions being advised that their portfolios should consist 100% of stocks and zero percent of bonds. This is quite a reversal from the earlier days when trust investments were restricted by law to high-grade bonds (and a few choice preferred stocks).
Aa inkaar na kari shakay tevaa tathyo par thi ghana financial jaankaro e nishkarsh kadhyo ke (1) bonds mool thi j kharab rokan chhe, ane (2) etle shares swabhav thi j bonds karta saru. Amae sambhalyu chhe ke charity sansthao ne salah apai ke temna portfolio 100% shares ane 0% bonds nu hovu joiye. Aa ghani ulti vaat chhe, pehla na samay thi, jyaare trust na rokan kanoon thi fakt uchcha darja na bonds (ane ketlaak chunda preferred shares) sudhi maryadit hata.

Professor Jeremy Siegel e kahyu ke "risk lenaar" investors e udhaar lai ne potani kul sampatti na 135% shares ma nakhva joiye! Ek sarkari adhikari e kahyu: "Koi e bond fund ma ek paisa na rakhvo." Paragraph 3: Graham no jawab: "Hamesha shares saru" ema ni

Our readers must have enough intelligence to recognize that even high-quality stocks cannot be a better purchase than bonds under all conditions--i.e., regardless of how high the stock market may be and how low the current dividend return compared with the rates available on bonds. A statement of this kind would be as absurd as was the contrary one--too often heard years ago--that any bond is safer than any stock.
Amara vachko ma etli samajdari hovi joiye ke saara darja na shares pan badhi paristhiti ma bonds karta vadhare saru kharid na hoi shake. Em ke bazaar bhale game tetlo uncho hoy, ane dividend yield bonds ni sarkhaman game tetli ochhi hoy. Aava vidhan hasyaspad chhe, jem ulatu vidhan (je ghanivar sambhalatu) ke "koi pan bond koi pan share karta safe chhe".

Paragraph 4: Ateet thi shikho (Table 2-1)

In this matter, as in so many others in finance, we must base our views of future policy on a knowledge of past experience. Is inflation something new for this country, at least in the serious form it has taken since 1965? If we have seen comparable (or worse) inflations in living experience, what lessons can be learned from them in confronting the inflation of today?
Aa vishay ma, nanaki na ghana bija vishayo ni jem, bhavishya ni niti mate amare ateet na anubhav na gnan par aadhar rakhvo joiye. Shu mehngai desh mate navi vaat chhe, kam se kam 1965 pachhi na gambhir swaroop ma? Jo ame jivit anubhav ma aava (ke vadhare kharab) mehngai na samay joyaa hoy, to aaj ni mehngai no saamno karva emathi shu shikhi shakay?

Paragraph 5: Pehlo nishkarsh: Mehngai pehla pan hati, ghani!

The first thing we notice is that we have had inflation in the past--lots of it. The largest five-year dose was between 1915 and 1920, when the cost of living nearly doubled. This compares with the advance of 15% between 1965 and 1970. In between, we have had three periods of declining prices and then six of advances at varying rates, some rather small. On this showing, the investor should clearly allow for the probability of continuing or recurrent inflation to come.
Sauthi pehli vaat dhyan ma aave chhe ke ateet ma pan mehngai hati, ghani. Sauthi moto 5 varsh no daaz 1915 thi 1920 vachche hato, jyare jivan-nirvaah no kharch lagbhag be gano thayo. Aa 1965 thi 1970 na 15% vadharo ni sarkhaman ma chhe. Vachche, 3 samay ma bhav ghatya ane 6 samay ma vadhya (ketlak ochha pramaan ma). Aa parinam par thi, investor e satat ke vaaramvar aavti mehngai ni shakyata spasht rite dhyan ma levi joiye.

Paragraph 6: Mehngai nu agaahi (anuman)

Can we tell what the rate of inflation is likely to be? No clear answer is suggested by our table; it shows variations of all sorts. It would seem sensible, however, to take our cue from the rather consistent record of the past 20 years. The average annual rise in the consumer price level for this period has been 2.5%; that for 1965-1970 was 4.5%; that for 1970 alone was 5.4%.
Shu ame kahi shakiye ke mehngai no dar kevo hase? Amari table spasht jawab nathi aapti; temaa badha prakar na pherfar chhe. Chhata, pacha na 20 varsh na kaaphi sarkha rekord par thi sanket levo vyajabi lage chhe. Aa gaala ma consumer price level nu sarerash varshik vadharo 2.5% hato; 1965-1970 nu 4.5%; fakt 1970 nu 5.4%.

🎯 Part 1 nu Saar

  1. Mehngai = bhav vadhe, paisa ni kharid-shakti ghate.
  2. Nakki aavak (bond, FD) ne nuksaan. Shares ne bachaav ni shakyata (dividend ane bhav vadhe).
  3. Ghana jaankaro kahe: "Bonds kharab, shares j saaru". Graham sahmat nathi: koi pan ek hamesha saaru nathi.
  4. Mehngai ateet ma pan hati (1915-20 ma kharch 2 gano). Pharithi aavshe.
  5. Pacha na 20 varsh ma sarerash mehngai 2.5%, pan vadhti jay chhe (1970 ma 5.4%).
